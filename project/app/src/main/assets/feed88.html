Feed88 Reader Guide

👋 Hello, I'm Mohamed

I built Feed 88 because I was tired of algorithms deciding what I read. No tracking, no "recommended for you", no ads. Just feeds I chose, delivered on my terms.

This guide teaches you two things: how to use the reader, and how to find RSS links that actually work — because honestly, that's the hardest part these days.

📖 How to use Feed 88

1
Add a feed

Tap Feeds → +, paste an RSS URL. Or tap the 📚 icon to browse 100 ready feeds.

2
Read

Tap any article. If the feed only sends a summary, Feed 88 fetches the full text automatically.

3
Organize

Star what matters, filter by Unread or Starred, mark all read when done.

4
Backup

Use Export OPML to save all feeds. Import them anywhere later.

💡 Everything is saved locally in your browser. Your data never leaves your device.

🔗 How to find working RSS links

Most sites hide their RSS link, and many "RSS URLs" floating around are dead. Here's my method, tested on hundreds of sites.

Method 1 — Look for the icon

Some sites still show an orange RSS icon in the footer or header. Click it, copy the URL, paste it here.

Method 2 — Try these common patterns

Add one of these suffixes to any site's home URL:

/feedWordPress default — most common
/rssClassic — used by many news sites
/rss.xmlOlder sites, CNN style
/feed.xmlAtom format — Blogger, modern sites
/atom.xmlAtom format — GitHub, blogs
/index.xmlHugo sites, personal blogs
/feed/atomBlogger-style
/.rssMedium, Substack

Example: for example.com, try example.com/feed, then example.com/rss, etc.

Method 3 — View the source

Open the site, right-click → View Page Source, search for rss+xml. You'll find:

<link rel="alternate" type="application/rss+xml" href="https://site.com/feed">

The href value is your RSS URL.

Method 4 — Use a generator

  • RSS.app — rss.app — feeds from almost any page
  • Feed43 — feed43.com — old-school, powerful for tricky sites
  • Politepol — politepol.com — visual feed builder

🎨 About the themes

Eight hand-tuned themes. Each has a mood:

  • Midnight — high contrast, late-night reading
  • Paper — clean white, bright rooms
  • Sepia — warm brown, easy on the eyes
  • Cyber — neon cyan, adventurous
  • Newspaper — old print feel, serif and ink
  • Chilling — soft green, low contrast, long sessions
  • Pink — soft rose, elegant
  • Purple — deep violet, mysterious

Tap the colored circle in the top bar to cycle through them.

📳 Vibration

Feed 88 vibrates subtly when you tap buttons — a small "I felt that" feedback. On phones it makes the app feel native. On desktop it does nothing (browsers can't vibrate).

To turn it on/off: tap the 📳 icon in the top bar. Your choice is remembered.
